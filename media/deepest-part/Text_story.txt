THE DEEPEST PART OF THE OCEAN A Fan-Made SpongeBob Story Written by Gio
Becerra

CHAPTER 1: THE MAP ON THE WAY TO WORK

SpongeBob SquarePants was having a perfectly normal morning, which, in
Bikini Bottom, was usually a warning that something strange was about to
happen. He bounced down the sidewalk toward the Krusty Krab with his
usual energy, carrying nothing but his lunch bag and a smile so large
that several passing fish wondered if he had received some extremely
good news. He had not. He was simply SpongeBob. “Today is going to be a
great day!” he announced to nobody in particular. “I can feel it!” He
stopped to straighten a crooked street sign, helped a tiny fish reach
something from a shelf outside a shop, and then continued on his way. He
was only a few blocks from the Krusty Krab when something caught his eye
near the edge of the sidewalk. It was a glass bottle, old and cloudy
from years underwater. SpongeBob picked it up and turned it over in his
hands. Something was inside. “Huh. Usually bottles contain messages,” he
said. “Or sand. Or somebody’s extremely disappointing lunch.” He pulled
the cork free and shook the bottle gently until a rolled piece of paper
slid into his hand. He carefully unrolled it. It was a map.

SpongeBob stared at it. The map showed parts of the ocean he had never
seen before. There were underwater mountains, twisting paths, strange
islands, and a long dark trench that seemed to stretch farther than
anything on an ordinary map. Along the edges were symbols SpongeBob
couldn’t recognize. At the very bottom was a black circle surrounded by
several markings. Underneath it, written in faded letters, were four
words: THE DEEPEST PART OF THE OCEAN. SpongeBob’s eyes widened. “The
deepest part?” He looked around as if someone might jump out and explain
it. Nobody did. A school of fish swam past without noticing. SpongeBob
rolled the map back up and slipped it into his pocket. “I’ll look at
this later,” he decided. Then he remembered something much more
important. “MY SHIFT!” He took off running toward the Krusty Krab,
leaving the mysterious map safely in his pocket and absolutely no idea
that his ordinary workday was about to become the beginning of the
biggest adventure of his life.

CHAPTER 2: SOMETHING VERY OLD

After his shift, SpongeBob rushed home and spread the map across his
table. He had spent the entire day thinking about it. Even while
flipping Krabby Patties, he had wondered what the symbols meant. Now,
under the light in his room, the details were easier to see. The map
wasn’t just showing a destination. It showed a route. A series of small
markings traveled from Bikini Bottom toward the edge of the known ocean,
eventually disappearing into the trench. SpongeBob traced the route with
his finger. “Why would somebody make a map to a place like this?” he
wondered. He grabbed a magnifying glass and leaned closer. Near the
bottom of the map was a tiny symbol that looked almost like an eye
surrounded by waves. Beneath it were words so faded that he could barely
read them: “Beyond the last light, the ocean remembers.”

SpongeBob sat back. “That’s either really important or extremely
dramatic.” He decided he couldn’t go alone. The map was too strange, and
whatever was at the end of it was too far away. He needed friends.
Unfortunately, choosing the right friends for a mysterious expedition
was harder than choosing what to eat for breakfast, especially when one
of those friends was Patrick. SpongeBob rolled the map up, put it into a
protective tube, and headed outside. He had no idea exactly what the
expedition would require, how long it would take, or whether the map was
even telling the truth. But he knew one thing for certain: he was going
to find out.

CHAPTER 3: ASSEMBLING THE CREW

The first person SpongeBob visited was Patrick. He found him sitting
outside his rock, staring at the ground with intense concentration.
“Patrick!” SpongeBob called. Patrick looked up. “SpongeBob!” “I found a
map to the deepest part of the ocean!” Patrick stared at him for several
seconds. “Is that farther away than the library?” SpongeBob blinked. “I
think so.” Patrick nodded seriously. “Then we should probably bring a
library.” SpongeBob laughed. “Do you want to come?” “Absolutely.”
Patrick stood up immediately. “I don’t know where we’re going, but I
already have a feeling I won’t understand most of it.” “That’s exactly
what I’m looking for,” SpongeBob said. They shook hands.

Next came Plankton. SpongeBob found him studying an old book in the Chum
Bucket. Plankton listened to the explanation, then demanded to see the
map. The second he saw the symbols, his expression changed. “Where did
you get this?” he asked. “I found it in a bottle.” “Do you know what
these symbols are?” “No.” Plankton climbed onto the map and examined it.
“These aren’t ordinary navigation marks. They’re old. Very old. I’ve
seen similar symbols in records that nobody takes seriously.” SpongeBob
leaned closer. “What do they say?” Plankton frowned. “Mostly things like
‘do not enter,’ ‘turn back,’ and ‘we probably should have labeled this
better.’” SpongeBob smiled. “So you’re coming?” Plankton hesitated. He
wanted to say no, but the mystery was too interesting. “Fine. But I’m
joining because I want answers.” “Great!” SpongeBob said. “And because
you’re small, you can fit in places we can’t.” Plankton’s eye narrowed.
“I am not going to be used as a measuring device.”

Mr. Krabs was next. SpongeBob showed him the map, and Mr. Krabs studied
it with a suspicious expression. “The deepest part of the ocean, eh?” he
said. “Sounds expensive.” SpongeBob explained that they had no idea what
they would find. Mr. Krabs immediately became interested when he noticed
a symbol that looked vaguely like a treasure chest. “I’m in.” “Really?”
“Aye. Any expedition involving an ancient map and an unexplored
destination has a reasonable chance of finding something valuable.”
“What if we don’t find treasure?” SpongeBob asked. Mr. Krabs paused.
“Then we’ll find something educational.” SpongeBob knew that meant
Mr. Krabs had absolutely no intention of leaving without something he
could sell.

The last person SpongeBob expected to meet was Harold. SpongeBob had
never really spoken to him before. Harold was a tall fish wearing an
enormous explorer’s hat, carrying binoculars, a notebook, and enough
pencils to suggest he expected the ocean to run out of them. He had
apparently been listening from nearby. “You’re going to the deepest part
of the ocean?” Harold asked. SpongeBob nodded. “I have experience with
expeditions.” Plankton looked at his equipment. “Have you actually been
anywhere?” Harold opened his notebook. “I’ve been to the other side of
town.” “That’s not an expedition,” Plankton said. “It took three buses.”
Harold closed the notebook. “It was a complicated expedition.” SpongeBob
smiled. “Do you want to come with us?” Harold considered this for
exactly three seconds. “Yes.” “Why?” “I’ve always wanted to see
something nobody has seen before.” SpongeBob grinned. “Then you’re in.”

There was only one person left. Squidward. SpongeBob knocked on his door
and explained the entire plan. Squidward listened silently, then stared
at the map. “No.” SpongeBob’s smile dropped. “You didn’t even let me
finish.” “You said ‘deepest part of the ocean.’ I heard enough.”
SpongeBob tried again. “There might be ancient secrets!” “Wonderful.”
“Unexplored places!” “Terrible.” “Adventure!” “Absolutely not.”
Squidward closed the door. SpongeBob stood there for a moment. “Well,”
he said, “I guess that’s a no.” From inside, Squidward shouted, “It’s a
very enthusiastic no!” SpongeBob returned to the others. Patrick,
Plankton, Mr. Krabs, and Harold were waiting. “Squidward isn’t coming,”
SpongeBob announced. Patrick shrugged. “He probably has important things
to do.” From somewhere inside Squidward’s house came the sound of a
clarinet. Harold wrote something in his notebook. “Subject has declined
expedition.” SpongeBob looked at the group. “All right. Then it’s
official. We’re going.”

CHAPTER 4: THE BOAT THAT SHOULD NOT HAVE FLOATED

Finding a boat turned out to be much harder than SpongeBob expected.
Mr. Krabs had an old vessel stored behind a warehouse, but it looked as
though it had spent several years losing arguments with the ocean. One
side was covered in patches. A window had been replaced with a piece of
cardboard. The steering wheel had a handwritten note taped to it that
said, “Good luck.” SpongeBob stared at it. “Are you sure this thing
works?” Mr. Krabs smiled. “Of course.” Plankton inspected the hull.
“That was not a convincing answer.” Harold climbed aboard and
immediately began taking notes. “Structural condition: concerning.
Captain: overly confident. Crew: questionable.” “Hey!” SpongeBob said.
Harold looked up. “I said questionable, not hopeless.” They loaded their
supplies, secured the map, and prepared to leave Bikini Bottom.

The journey began smoothly enough. The boat moved away from the familiar
buildings and streets, carrying the group into water that gradually
became darker and quieter. SpongeBob stood at the front, holding the map
tube. Patrick watched the horizon. Plankton checked the instruments.
Mr. Krabs held the wheel. Harold wrote in his notebook. “This is
amazing,” SpongeBob said. “We’re actually doing it.” Patrick nodded. “I
can’t believe we’re leaving Bikini Bottom.” Plankton looked over the
side. “Enjoy the view while it lasts.” The map suddenly trembled in
SpongeBob’s hands. A new line appeared across the paper, glowing
faintly. SpongeBob’s smile disappeared. “Guys?” Everyone turned. The
route on the map had changed. A path that hadn’t existed before was now
leading them toward a section of the ocean marked with a single word:
BELOW.

CHAPTER 5: BEYOND THE LAST LIGHT

They continued until Bikini Bottom was completely out of sight. The
water around them had become darker, and the usual sounds of the ocean
had faded away. Even the fish had disappeared. SpongeBob looked down
into the water and could no longer see the bottom. “This is where the
map said ‘beyond the last light,’” he said quietly. Harold checked his
notebook. “Technically, there is still light.” “That’s your
observation?” Plankton asked. “It’s an important observation.” Suddenly,
the boat’s lights flickered. Then they went out. For a few seconds,
nobody said anything. Mr. Krabs slowly reached for a switch. Nothing
happened. “I knew I should have charged these,” he muttered. Patrick
pointed ahead. “Look.”

Far below the boat, a faint blue glow moved through the darkness. It
wasn’t a fish. It wasn’t a submarine. It was a trail of lights, arranged
in a perfect line, stretching down into the depths. SpongeBob held up
the map. The glowing route on the paper matched the lights below.
“That’s where we’re supposed to go.” Mr. Krabs stared over the side.
“Down?” SpongeBob nodded. “Down.” Plankton looked at the boat. “Our boat
does not go down.” A moment later, the entire vessel began sinking.
Everyone stared at Plankton. “You had to say it,” Harold said. “I have
noticed that sentences spoken immediately before disasters are often
poorly timed.” SpongeBob grabbed the controls. The boat descended
slowly, following the strange blue lights. Instead of breaking apart,
the vessel somehow held together. The deeper they went, the more
enormous the ocean became around them. Shapes moved in the distance, too
far away to identify. Then something enormous passed beneath the boat,
and the entire vessel lifted slightly in the water.

Nobody spoke. Even Patrick was silent. Finally Harold wrote in his
notebook: “Something large. Very large. Recommend not annoying it.”

CHAPTER 6: THE ROAD UNDER THE SEA

At the bottom of the descent, the blue lights led them toward a wide
stone path. It stretched across the seafloor like an ancient road.
Strange pillars stood along both sides, each carved with the same
eye-shaped symbol from the map. SpongeBob stared in amazement. “Somebody
built this.” Plankton climbed over the side and examined one of the
pillars. “This isn’t just old. It’s ancient.” Mr. Krabs looked around.
“And nobody has claimed it yet.” Plankton sighed. “That is the part of
the sentence you’re focusing on?” Harold walked around the pillar,
taking measurements. “The craftsmanship is remarkable.” Patrick looked
at the road. “Where does it go?” SpongeBob pointed ahead. “Straight into
that mountain.”

The road disappeared into a massive opening in the rock. The group
brought the boat through the entrance and found themselves inside a
cavern far larger than anything they had expected. The walls were
covered in carvings showing strange underwater cities, enormous
creatures, and groups of fish standing around a glowing circle. At the
center of every carving was the same symbol. SpongeBob moved his
flashlight across the wall. “It’s telling a story.” Plankton studied the
pictures. “It looks like this place was built around something.”
“Something valuable?” Mr. Krabs asked. “Something important.” “Same
thing, depending on your definition.” SpongeBob laughed. They followed
the road deeper into the cavern until they reached a huge stone gate.
Above it was an inscription. Plankton slowly translated it. “Beyond this
gate lies what the ocean chose to hide.” Patrick frowned. “Why would the
ocean hide something?” Harold closed his notebook. “That is an excellent
question.” SpongeBob looked at the gate. “Let’s find out.”

CHAPTER 7: THE CITY BELOW

The gate opened with a deep rumble, revealing a city hidden beneath the
ocean floor. Towers rose from the darkness. Bridges crossed enormous
open spaces. Windows glowed faintly with blue light even though nobody
seemed to live there. The streets were perfectly preserved, as though
the city had been abandoned only yesterday. SpongeBob stepped onto the
stone road. “This is incredible.” Mr. Krabs stared at the buildings.
“Imagine the property value.” Plankton ignored him and ran toward a
large structure at the center of the city. “There! That building is
covered in the same symbols.” The others followed.

Inside, they discovered a huge circular chamber. In the middle stood a
machine unlike anything they had ever seen. It had no obvious controls,
only a ring of glowing symbols surrounding a dark center. SpongeBob
approached carefully. “What do you think it does?” Plankton studied it.
“I don’t know.” Patrick looked around. “Maybe it’s a very complicated
door.” Harold pointed to the floor. “There are five spaces around the
machine.” SpongeBob counted them. “Five.” He looked at the crew. “And
there are five of us.” Everyone became quiet. “That’s probably not a
coincidence,” SpongeBob said.

One by one, they stood in the spaces. SpongeBob placed his hand on the
first symbol. Patrick placed his hand on the second. Plankton climbed
onto the third. Mr. Krabs stood at the fourth. Harold took the fifth.
The machine suddenly came alive. Blue light spread through the chamber,
and the dark center opened like a window. Inside it was not another
room. It was an image of the ocean itself, stretching downward farther
than the map had shown. At the very bottom was a point of light. A voice
echoed through the chamber: “You have reached the beginning.”

Patrick looked at SpongeBob. “I thought we were reaching the end.”
SpongeBob stared at the glowing point. “Maybe the deepest part isn’t the
end of the ocean.” Plankton slowly smiled. “It’s the beginning of
something else.”

CHAPTER 8: THE KEEPER OF THE DEEP

The chamber shook. A figure appeared in the blue light. It wasn’t a
ghost or a monster. It was an ancient-looking explorer wearing a damaged
diving suit and carrying a staff covered in symbols. “Who are you?”
SpongeBob asked. The figure looked at them. “I am the Keeper.” Mr. Krabs
immediately asked, “Keeper of what?” The Keeper pointed toward the
opening in the floor. “The passage to the deepest place.” Plankton
stepped forward. “Why hide it?” The Keeper answered, “Because the
deepest ocean is not merely water. It is where the oldest things are
remembered.”

SpongeBob listened carefully. The Keeper explained that long ago,
explorers had reached the bottom and discovered a place where the
ocean’s history seemed to collect. Every current, every forgotten path,
and every ancient civilization left something behind. But the place was
too powerful to be turned into a treasure vault or a shortcut. The
ancient explorers built the city and sealed the passage. “Then why did
the map lead us here?” SpongeBob asked. The Keeper looked at the map.
“Because the map was never meant to keep people away.” “What was it
meant to do?” “To find people willing to look deeper.”

Harold quietly raised his hand. “I have a question.” The Keeper waited.
“Do we have to pass a test?” “Yes.” Harold nodded. “I suspected as
much.” A large stone door opened behind the Keeper. Beyond it was a
staircase leading downward. “Only those who understand why they came may
continue.” Patrick looked at SpongeBob. “I came because you asked.”
Mr. Krabs said, “I came because I thought there might be treasure.”
Plankton crossed his arms. “I came for knowledge.” Harold said, “I came
because I wanted to see something nobody had seen before.” The Keeper
looked at SpongeBob. “And you?” SpongeBob smiled. “I came because I
found a mystery, and I wanted to understand it.” The Keeper stepped
aside. “Then continue.”

CHAPTER 9: THE FIVE CHOICES

The staircase seemed to go down forever. At different points, the group
encountered doors with symbols above them. Each door presented a
different challenge, but none required strength. The first showed
SpongeBob a perfect picture of Bikini Bottom and asked him to turn back.
He almost did, but he remembered that the point of the expedition was to
discover something new. The door opened. The second challenge confused
Patrick with several paths that looked identical. Patrick stared at
them, then walked straight through a wall that wasn’t real. Everyone
else followed him after realizing the walls were illusions. “How did you
know?” SpongeBob asked. Patrick shrugged. “It looked like the kind of
thing that would be fake.” Plankton stared at him. “That is possibly the
strangest useful explanation I’ve ever heard.”

The third challenge was for Plankton. A chamber contained hundreds of
books, but only one held the correct information. Plankton searched
through them, becoming increasingly frustrated. Finally, he stopped
trying to prove he was the smartest person in the room and simply read
the labels. The correct book was sitting on a shelf marked “READ THIS
ONE.” “I hate this place,” he muttered. Mr. Krabs’s challenge involved a
room filled with objects that appeared valuable. He had to leave every
one behind. He stared at a golden statue for almost a full minute before
sighing. “This hurts.” Harold’s challenge was the strangest. He entered
a room with a sign that said, “Write down what you know.” Harold opened
his notebook and began writing. After several minutes, he closed it. “I
don’t know enough,” he said. The door opened. Harold looked surprised.
“That worked?” SpongeBob smiled. “Maybe the test wanted you to admit
it.” Harold nodded thoughtfully. “I will write that down.”

At last they reached the final door. SpongeBob placed the map against
it. The paper dissolved into light, and the door opened. Beyond it was
an enormous vertical shaft extending farther downward than any of them
could see. In the center was a narrow platform leading toward a glowing
point at the bottom. “That’s it,” SpongeBob whispered. “The deepest
part.”

CHAPTER 10: THE DEEPEST PART

They descended for what felt like hours. The walls disappeared into
darkness. The water became completely still. There were no currents, no
sounds, and no creatures. The only thing visible was the glowing point
below them. Eventually, they reached the bottom. It was not a treasure
room. It was a vast open chamber beneath the ocean floor, surrounded by
smooth stone walls. In the center floated a small sphere of light.
SpongeBob walked toward it. As he approached, images appeared around the
chamber: ancient cities, forgotten explorers, enormous reefs, storms,
shipwrecks, and countless moments from the history of the ocean. They
weren’t frightening. They were memories.

Plankton stared in amazement. “This is an archive.” Harold shook his
head. “More than an archive. It’s a record of everything that happened
here.” Mr. Krabs looked at the glowing sphere. “So there’s no treasure?”
SpongeBob looked at him. “Mr. Krabs.” “I’m only asking.” Patrick reached
toward one of the images but stopped before touching it. “It’s kind of
beautiful.” SpongeBob smiled. The Keeper appeared behind them. “This is
why the city was built. The deepest part was never meant to be owned. It
was meant to be remembered.”

SpongeBob looked around the chamber and finally understood the message
on the map. The deepest part of the ocean wasn’t just the lowest point.
It was a place where the ocean kept the things that had been forgotten
everywhere else. “We found it,” SpongeBob said. The Keeper nodded. “And
now you must decide what to do with what you have seen.” SpongeBob
thought about it. He could tell everyone about the city. They could
become famous. Mr. Krabs could probably sell something. Plankton could
spend the rest of his life studying it. But the place had been hidden
for a reason. “We should leave it alone,” SpongeBob said. “We can
remember what we saw without turning it into something it isn’t.” The
Keeper smiled. “You understand.”

CHAPTER 11: THE RIDE HOME

The journey back was much quieter than the journey there. The ancient
city remained behind them, hidden beneath layers of ocean and stone. The
blue lights guided their boat back through the cavern and toward the
surface. Along the way, Patrick stared out into the water. “You know
what I learned?” he asked. “What?” SpongeBob said. Patrick thought for a
moment. “The ocean is really big.” Plankton sighed. “That’s what you
learned?” “I also learned that maps don’t always show everything.”
SpongeBob smiled. “That’s actually pretty good.” Harold wrote it down.
“Patrick has produced a philosophical statement.” Patrick looked
pleased. “I don’t know what that means, but it sounds important.”

When the boat finally returned to familiar waters, Bikini Bottom
appeared in the distance. SpongeBob felt relieved to see home again.
They had been gone long enough for the town to feel strangely new.
Mr. Krabs checked the supplies and complained about how much the
expedition had cost. Plankton had filled several notebooks with
observations. Harold had filled nearly an entire notebook with drawings,
measurements, and notes. Patrick had somehow brought back a small stone
from the city, but when everyone looked at it, it turned out to be an
ordinary pebble from the boat. “I thought it looked ancient,” Patrick
said. “It fell out of the engine.” “Still,” Harold said, “it had a
journey.”

SpongeBob rolled up the map. For the first time, the paper looked
completely blank. The route, the symbols, and the words had all
disappeared. SpongeBob smiled. “Maybe the map was only meant to exist
until somebody found the place.” Plankton nodded. “That would make
sense.” Mr. Krabs frowned. “I still think it should have included
directions to treasure.” SpongeBob laughed. “Maybe that was never the
point.”

CHAPTER 12: SQUidWARD HEARS THE STORY

When SpongeBob returned home, the first thing he did was visit
Squidward. He knocked on the door. Squidward opened it with an annoyed
expression. “You’re back.” “Yep!” SpongeBob said. “We found it.”
Squidward raised an eyebrow. “Found what?” “The deepest part of the
ocean.” Squidward stared at him. “You actually went?” “All the way
down.” SpongeBob spent the next several minutes explaining the ancient
city, the hidden road, the Keeper, the strange machine, and the glowing
archive of ocean memories. Squidward listened without interrupting. When
SpongeBob finished, Squidward looked toward his clarinet. “Sounds
exhausting.” SpongeBob nodded. “It was.” “Dangerous?” “Sometimes.”
“Strange?” “Very.” Squidward sighed. “And you brought back nothing?”
SpongeBob smiled. “Just the story.”

Squidward looked at him for a moment. “Well… that’s something.”
SpongeBob grinned. “Do you wish you had come?” Squidward immediately
answered, “No.” There was a pause. “Maybe a little.” SpongeBob laughed.
“I knew it!” “Don’t get excited.” Squidward closed the door, but this
time he didn’t slam it. Inside, he sat down and stared at the wall,
thinking about everything SpongeBob had described. Outside, SpongeBob
walked home, happy that his friends had shared an adventure nobody else
had ever experienced.

That night, SpongeBob placed the empty map tube on his shelf. He
expected that to be the end of it. But just before he went to sleep,
something strange happened. The tube began to glow. SpongeBob opened his
eyes. A tiny piece of paper had appeared inside. He pulled it out and
unfolded it. There was only one sentence written on it: “There are
places deeper than the deepest.”

SpongeBob stared at the message. Then he smiled.

“Maybe there’s another adventure after all.”

THE END… FOR NOW
